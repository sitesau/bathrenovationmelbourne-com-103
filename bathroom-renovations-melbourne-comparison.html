Cost Drivers

The published Melbourne range, about $8,000 to $35,000 for a full renovation, is broad enough to warn against treating one headline number as the answer. The source also cites recent Housing Industry Association data at around $26,000 as an average Australian spend. Both figures are useful, but neither replaces a proper reading of what the contractor has priced. The source ties cost movement to room size, layout changes and the quality of fixtures and finishes. It also says labour usually takes a large share of the total, especially where waterproofing, plumbing or electrical work is involved.

When two numbers seem far apart, check whether they are pricing the same layout, the same service work, and the same finish level. If one proposal assumes no layout change and another includes service relocation, they are not direct comparisons. A higher quote is not automatically inflated. It may simply include more of the real job, such as licensed plumbing work or complex waterproofing systems required by building codes.

Home Type Factors

Home type changes the quoting risk. Apartments, terraces, period homes, family houses and compact bathrooms are not cosmetic labels. They affect access, layout efficiency and the kind of issues that surface before work starts. Its own examples are specific enough to guide questions. In an apartment, the early question is often about access or approvals. In a compact ensuite, the main issue may be storage, ventilation and circulation. In a larger family bathroom, the pressure may shift toward fixture quality once the services and layout are settled.

Apartment job: ask what building or access details are needed before the quote is firm. Compact room: ask which layout change improves clearance or storage most. Linked spaces: ask whether the bathroom, laundry or powder-room work is being priced as one coordinated scope. These distinctions ensure you are comparing like with like when reviewing proposals from general contractors.

The Planning Sequence

One of the strongest planning cues on the source page is sequence. Wet-area compliance comes first, and waterproofing, plumbing and electrical scope should be reviewed before fit-off decisions are locked in. That order keeps the technical parts of the room visible while the project is still being defined. Many homeowners naturally focus on finishes because they are easier to picture. Quotes can then look detailed without actually clarifying the hard parts.

If the wet area, plumbing path or electrical work is under-described, a polished fixtures list does not fix the scope problem. It only shifts attention away from it. The renovation checklist suits this stage because it helps organise the brief before you commit to a quote comparison. Written exclusions and quote assumptions matter for the same reason. They show whether the contractor is pricing a straightforward replacement, a redesign, or a room with access and compliance friction already built into it.

  1. Identify the specific room problem. Determine if the issue is leaking, cramped layout, poor ventilation or outdated fixtures before requesting quotes.
  2. Assess access and approvals. Check if the bathroom is in an apartment or terrace where owners corporation approval or access restrictions will affect the scope.
  3. Request a written scope. Ask each contractor to outline the waterproofing, plumbing and electrical work included in the price, not just the fixtures.
  4. Compare quotes based on scope. Ensure all proposals cover the same layout changes, service relocations and finish level before comparing the total price.
